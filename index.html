Research agenda

Secure identity at scale

My work focuses on scalable and trustworthy biometric systems for human-centered security, privacy-preserving identity verification, Bloom filter-based authentication, and secure data management.

My Ph.D. dissertation, Hierarchical Probabilistic Architectures for Scalable Biometric and Electronic Authentication in Secure Surveillance, develops a unified family of Hierarchical Bloom Filter architectures for biometric and hardware identity systems operating under noise, large population growth, and constrained storage.

Doctoral work

Three research pillars

01

Dynamic Hierarchical Bloom Filters

Noise-tolerant, dynamically updatable probabilistic structures for large-scale facial biometric enrollment, query, insertion, and deletion.

30,000 facial templates evaluated

02

Bio-BloomChain

Privacy-preserving biometric lifecycle management that combines hierarchical probabilistic verification with smart contracts and non-invertible commitments.

<$0.001 reported enrollment/deletion cost on Polygon zkEVM

03

Persistent HBF for Hardware

Noise-resilient authentication of electronic fingerprints generated from physical unclonable functions for secure semiconductor tracking.

105× faster query processing reported vs. large-scale blockchain tracking
